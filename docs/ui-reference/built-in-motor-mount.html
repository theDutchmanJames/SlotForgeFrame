How it is put together

  • One new choice, in Pod › Base plate: how the motor mount is held. Separate pod is what Frame makes now: a pod in an opening, screwed to the chassis, free to float. Built into the chassis makes one part, as on Plafit- and Schöler-style metal chassis: the motor bracket and the rear bearing uprights stand on the plate itself.
  • Everything else stays as it is. The Pod menu still sets the layout (inline, sidewinder, anglewinder and its angle), the motor, the gears, the motor seat and the bearing uprights, with every bearing in the library (6 mm for 3 mm axles, flanged, eccentric, shoulder seats). Only the mount lugs and the screw holes go, as there is nothing to screw on, and the export is one STL.
  • The chassis stays closed round the motor and the gear, as it is now: the plate is carried round them, and the motor and the gear keep their room where they come through it.
  • Front axle in bearings: Features › Front axle gets a second kind of upright beside the bracket: two uprights with a bearing seat, picked from the same bearing library as the rear (a 6 mm bearing for a 3 mm axle on a 1/24 car). Flanged bearings rest against the outside face. The axle height follows the front tyres.
  • Checks as now, and two more: a built-in mount needs the plate under the motor and the uprights to be solid (the fill leaves pads there), and a front bearing must fit in its upright with a wall round it.

Ideas beyond the brief

  • A switch back and forth costs nothing: draw the car with a separate pod, then print it in one piece, or the other way round.
  • Front bearing uprights can also take the eccentric bushings already in the library, to set the front ride height.
