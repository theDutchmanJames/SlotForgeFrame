How the pattern tools work

  • An insert is a base plus layers. The base is the disc (or a cup with a skirt that slides into the wheel), its dish, rim lip and centre hole. Each layer adds one kind of pattern on top.
  • Every layer repeats round the centre by itself. You set a count; the app draws one and copies it round, so a pattern is always even. The "One segment" view shows the wedge you are actually shaping.
  • Every layer is cut through, sunk in or raised, with its own depth or height. Five spokes are a "cut through" spokes layer; a raised centre nut is a "raise" layer.
  • Five layer types cover most real wheels: Spokes (straight, curved, split, crossed into a mesh), Holes (round, slot or window, in a ring), Ring (a step or groove), Bolts & nut (studs on a circle and a hex or centre-lock nut) and Draw your own (draw one segment with the outline tools, or import an SVG; it is repeated like the rest).
  • Fit comes from the wheel: the pocket diameter and depth in your aluminium wheel, plus a clearance, decide the insert's size. A calibration ring prints the insert's outside in steps of 0.05 mm, like the chassis coupon does for holes.
  • Checks: spokes or bars thinner than your nozzle can print, a pattern that leaves the insert in loose pieces, holes that run into each other, a centre hole smaller than the axle or grub screw access, and an insert thicker than the pocket is deep.
  • Print: a set of four (or two front and two rear), face down on the plate so the face comes out smooth. Directional patterns (curved or turbine) are mirrored for the other side of the car.
